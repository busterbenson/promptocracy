# Rules voice

Buster, 2026-11-19: "crisp board-game rulebook", and "use the player's own words whenever it can be done in a clear way".

1. Talk to the player: "you", present tense. "You can move one tile a day."
2. One idea per sentence. Say what you can do first, then the limit: "You can withdraw your proposal until midnight, unless you've ended your turn."
3. Everyday words. "can", not "may" or "shall". No legalese: hereby, pursuant to, in the event that, aforementioned, notwithstanding.
4. Keep the game's own terms, the same every time: moderator, proposal, draft, ballot, block, mark, clause, turn, day, tile, piece.
5. Digits for numbers (3, not three). Cite rules by number in parentheses: (202.4).
6. A player's own words stay when they are clear, fit the rule's terms and say exactly what they mean. Reword only to make it exact, to match a game term, or to fix the grammar.
7. Nothing in a rule that isn't a rule: no flavor text, no asides, no "note that". The fun is in what the rules let you do.
8. Say each thing once. Every number, condition, consequence, list and exception lives in one clause. Any other clause that needs it cites that clause by number instead of saying it again: "When a player wins the Era (402.2), the Era ends that night", not "The first player to reach 100 points wins, and the Era ends that night." A short name with a citation is fine ("the lock (107.1)", "the Champion's change (402.4)"); repeating what that clause says is not. The test: if that other clause changed, would this one become wrong? Then it is repeating it. An exception to a core rule is written in the core rule, and the rule that makes the exception doesn't repeat it. A setting's number appears once, next to its tag.

Point 8 is guidance, not a rule. A player may break it and live with the consequences: a restated fact can drift out of step with its owner, and then the rules disagree (rule 108). The moderators try to prevent it: Starling when she drafts, and Chalant when it applies a draft or redrafts one (rule 107.3). The rulebook check does not reject a restatement.

Where text goes. A rule has no text of its own (rule 109.1): a new rule's text is its first clause, N.1. Before day 1 a rule's clauses are numbered from 1 with no gaps; once play starts a repealed clause leaves its number empty.

Before: "The proposer may withdraw a confirmed proposal until it goes on the ballot, unless they have ended their turn (rule 202.4). A withdrawn proposal doesn't use up the day's proposal."
After: "You can withdraw your proposal until midnight, unless you've ended your turn (202.4). Withdrawing frees up your proposal for the day."
